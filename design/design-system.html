Gadget Anatomy Plan, 21 November 2026

Design system and key screens

A web app for first-year ICT students that opens up the phone, laptop and PC they already use, in real 3D, and follows everyday actions from a finger out to a data centre. It wears Archboard's look, with larger reading text and one colour for each job a part does.

CourseCMFP0060 ICT, year 1, about 90 students
Used onlab PCs (one monitor), phones, the lecture projector
FamilyArchboard's tokens and outlines; a separate app
3DThree.js 0.170.0; parts built to their published sizes
StatusPlan only. Nothing is built.

Direction

The course's own frame

Every part is tagged with its job in the model Lecture 2 teaches: input, process, output, storage. Memory (Lecture 3), communicate and connect and power complete it.

Real, then simple

Parts are drawn to their published sizes and shown beside a real photo. Then two plain sentences and one example from a student's day.

Two scales, one look

Inside the device is 3D. Outside, the room, the provider and the data centre use Archboard's isometric blocks. A journey crosses both without a change of style.

Works on every machine

No WebGL, an old phone or a busy projector gets the same content as a photo with labels. Keyboard, reduced motion and both themes everywhere.

Roles: one colour for each job

The same colour means the same job on every device. A role word is always written next to its colour, never colour alone.

Type

Archboard's two faces. Every sentence is in IBM Plex Sans at 17 px for year-1 readers; the interface, part names and labels are in JetBrains Mono. The projector sizes are read from the back of the room.

Step 5 of 9

A DNS server finds the address

Computers find each other by numbers, not names. Your internet provider asks a DNS server for the number that belongs to www.itc.edu.kh, and the request goes on to that number.

DNS serverInternet provider

Steps: you type and press Enter; the CPU hands the request to the Wi-Fi card; radio to the router; the router to your provider; DNS finds the number; the web server reads the page from its storage; the page travels back; the GPU draws it; you see it.

Screen E: on a phone

The same viewer at 390 px wide: the model fills the screen and the part card is a bottom sheet. Parts are chips the student swipes through.

Smartphone

Small screens

  • The model takes the whole width; one finger turns it, two fingers zoom.
  • Parts are a row of chips; the selected one's card rises as a sheet and can be dragged up to read more.
  • The phone model is a generic phone, not one brand's measurements, and says so.
  • Under reduced motion, nothing turns by itself and Take apart jumps straight to the apart view.
  • Without WebGL the Photo view opens instead, with the same parts as numbered pins.
